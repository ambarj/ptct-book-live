The Drunkard’s Walk

A drunkard stands at a lamppost. Each second, they take one step — equally likely left or right. Where will they be after 100 steps? After 1,000? No one can predict a single trajectory, but statistics can predict the ensemble. This simple model — the random walk — is the foundation of diffusion, Brownian motion, stock prices, and polymer physics.

Learning Objectives
  • Simulate a 1D random walk: at each step, move +1 (right) or −1 (left) with probability \( p \) and \( q = 1 - p \)
  • Observe that individual trajectories are unpredictable, but ensembles have clear statistical patterns
  • Understand the unbiased walk (\( p = q = 1/2 \)): zero mean displacement, growing spread
  • See how bias (\( p \neq 1/2 \)) creates a drift in the average position

The 1D Random Walk:

$$ x_N = \sum_{i=1}^{N} s_i, \qquad s_i = \begin{cases} +1 & \text{with probability } p \\ -1 & \text{with probability } q = 1-p \end{cases} $$

After \( N \) steps, the position is the sum of \( N \) independent random steps. For the unbiased walk (\( p = 1/2 \)), the expected position is zero, but the typical distance from the origin grows as \( \sqrt{N} \).

Walk 100 steps, then click New Walk and walk 100 again. Are the two trajectories the same?
Explanation: Each walk uses independent random coin flips, so the trajectory is completely different each time. This is the fundamental unpredictability of random walks. The final position varies wildly between walks — but the statistics (mean, variance) are predictable.
Take several walks of 100 steps each. The histogram (right panel) shows where walks end up. Where is the histogram centered?
Explanation: For the unbiased walk (\( p = 1/2 \)), right and left steps are equally likely. On average, they cancel out: \( \langle x_N \rangle = 0 \). The histogram is centered at zero, but has a spread of about \( \pm\sqrt{N} = \pm 10 \) for 100 steps.
After 100 unbiased steps, the typical distance from the origin is about \( \sqrt{N} = \sqrt{100} = \) . (Check the spread of the histogram)
Explanation: The root-mean-square displacement is \( \sqrt{\langle x^2 \rangle} = \sqrt{N} = 10 \). Most walks end within about ±10 of the origin after 100 steps. Not ±100 (the walker doesn’t keep going in one direction) and not 0 (they don’t return exactly). The \( \sqrt{N} \) law is universal.

Test Your Understanding

Use the Many Walkers explorer to answer these questions.

Launch 200 walkers for 200 steps. The envelope of trajectories forms a shape. What shape is it?
Explanation: The spread grows as \( \sqrt{N} \), forming a widening funnel (diffusion cone). This is the visual signature of diffusion: the cloud of walkers expands, but slowly — as the square root of time, not linearly.
Look at the histogram of final positions. What distribution does it resemble?
Explanation: By the central limit theorem, the sum of many independent random steps converges to a Gaussian (normal) distribution. The mean is 0 (unbiased) and the standard deviation is \( \sqrt{N} \). This is the mathematical foundation of diffusion.
Which statements about the unbiased random walk are true? (Select all)
Explanation:
  • Mean = 0: ✓ Unbiased walk has zero expected displacement.
  • Always returns: ✗ In 1D, a random walk eventually returns (Pólya’s recurrence), but not at every step. Individual walks wander far.
  • RMS ∝ N: ✗ RMS grows as √N, not N. Linear growth would mean ballistic motion.
  • RMS ∝ √N: ✓ This is the diffusion law: \( \sqrt{\langle x^2 \rangle} = \sqrt{N} \).
Explorer: The Biased Drunkard

What if the street is on a slope? If right steps are more likely (\( p > 1/2 \)), the walker drifts. The mean position shifts linearly with time: \( \langle x_N \rangle = N(2p - 1) \). But fluctuations still grow as \( \sqrt{N} \), riding on top of the drift.

Set p = 0.55 (slight rightward bias). After 300 steps, where is the cloud of walkers centered?
Explanation: The mean displacement is \( \langle x_N \rangle = N(2p - 1) = 300 \times 0.1 = 30 \). The walker drifts rightward at a rate of \( 2p - 1 = 0.1 \) per step. The cloud is centered at +30, with a spread of \( \pm\sqrt{300} \approx \pm 17 \).

Python Code

Python 

import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go

# Single random walk
N = 500
steps = np.random.choice([-1, 1], N)
position = np.cumsum(steps)

fig = go.Figure()
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(
    y=position, mode='lines',
    line=dict(color='#00f3ff', width=1.5),
    name='Walker trajectory'))
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(
    x=[0, N], y=[0, 0], mode='lines',
    line=dict(color='#808080', width=1, dash='dot'),
    showlegend=False))

fig.update_layout(
    template='plotly_dark',
    xaxis=dict(title='Step number', zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    yaxis=dict(title='Position x', zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    height=400, width=600)
fig.show()
print(f'Final position: {position[-1]}')
print(f'Expected RMS: √{N} = {np.sqrt(N):.1f}')
Python 

import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go
from plotly.subplots import make_subplots

n_walkers, N = 200, 500
positions = np.cumsum(np.random.choice([-1, 1], (n_walkers, N)), axis=1)

fig = make_subplots(1, 2, subplot_titles=['Trajectories', 'Final positions'])

colors = ['#00f3ff','#ff006e','#ffbe0b','#00ff88','#bb86fc']
for w in range(min(n_walkers, 20)):
    fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(
        y=positions[w], mode='lines',
        line=dict(color=colors[w % 5], width=0.8, opacity=0.5),
        showlegend=False), row=1, col=1)

# ±√N envelope
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(
    x=list(range(N)), y=[np.sqrt(n) for n in range(N)],
    mode='lines', line=dict(color='white', width=2, dash='dash'),
    name='±√N'), row=1, col=1)
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(
    x=list(range(N)), y=[-np.sqrt(n) for n in range(N)],
    mode='lines', line=dict(color='white', width=2, dash='dash'),
    showlegend=False), row=1, col=1)

# Histogram
fig.add_trace(go.Histogram(
    x=positions[:, -1], nbinsx=30,
    marker_color='#00f3ff', opacity=0.7), row=1, col=2)

fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark', height=400, width=900)
fig.update_xaxes(title='Step', zerolinecolor='#808080', row=1, col=1)
fig.update_yaxes(title='Position', zerolinecolor='#808080', row=1, col=1)
fig.update_xaxes(title='Final position', zerolinecolor='#808080', row=1, col=2)
fig.show()
