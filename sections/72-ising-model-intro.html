Ising Model Introduction (Optional)

Monte Carlo was born to solve physics problems. The Ising model is the perfect example: a chain of tiny magnets (spins) that can point up or down. The energy depends on how neighbors align. At low temperature, spins order; at high temperature, they scramble. Computing thermal averages requires summing over all configurations — a task that becomes impossible for large systems. This is where Metropolis Monte Carlo (next section) saves the day.

Learning Objectives
  • Understand the 1D Ising model: spins \( s_i = \pm 1 \), nearest-neighbor interaction energy
  • Compute the energy \( E = -J\sum s_i s_{i+1} \) for any spin configuration
  • Understand Boltzmann weights \( e^{-E/k_BT} \) and how temperature controls order vs disorder
  • See why brute-force enumeration of all \( 2^N \) configurations is impossible for large \( N \)

The 1D Ising Model:

$$ E = -J\sum_{i=1}^{N-1} s_i\, s_{i+1}, \qquad s_i = \pm 1 $$

Each spin \( s_i \) is either +1 (up) or −1 (down). Aligned neighbors (\( s_i = s_{i+1} \)) contribute \( -J \) (lower energy). Anti-aligned neighbors contribute \( +J \) (higher energy). The system prefers alignment.

Thermal average: \( \langle A \rangle = \frac{1}{Z}\sum_{\text{configs}} A\, e^{-E/k_BT} \), where \( Z = \sum e^{-E/k_BT} \) is the partition function.

Reading the Energy

Each pair of neighbors contributes \( -J \) if aligned or \( +J \) if anti-aligned. With N spins there are N−1 pairs. The minimum energy is \( -(N-1)J \) (all aligned). The maximum is \( +(N-1)J \) (alternating). A random configuration has energy near zero on average.

Explorer: Temperature and Boltzmann Weights

Temperature controls order. At \( T = 0 \): only the ground state matters (perfect order). At \( T \to \infty \): all configurations are equally likely (complete disorder). The Boltzmann weight \( e^{-E/k_BT} \) interpolates between these extremes. Watch the bar chart of weights change as you adjust temperature.

Explorer: Why Brute Force Fails

For small N, we can enumerate all \( 2^N \) configurations and compute exact thermal averages. But \( 2^N \) grows exponentially: \( 2^{10} = 1{,}024 \), \( 2^{20} \approx 10^6 \), \( 2^{30} \approx 10^9 \). By \( N = 50 \), there are more configurations than atoms in the Earth. This is why we need Monte Carlo — which we’ll build in the next section.

For N = 10 spins, the total number of configurations is \( 2^{10} = \) .
Explanation: Each of 10 spins can be +1 or −1 independently, giving \( 2^{10} = 1{,}024 \) configurations. Brute-force enumeration is easy here. But at N = 30: \( 2^{30} \approx 10^9 \) — already a billion. At N = 100: \( 2^{100} \approx 10^{30} \) — totally impossible.
Set N = 12 and T/J = 0.5. The energy histogram shows that most configurations have high energy, but the weighted average is dominated by…
Explanation: At low T, the Boltzmann weight \( e^{-E/k_BT} \) is exponentially larger for low-energy states. Even though there are more high-energy configurations, the few low-energy ones have such enormous weight that they dominate the thermal average. This competition between energy and entropy is the heart of statistical mechanics.
The Metropolis algorithm (next section) solves the enumeration problem by…
Explanation: The Metropolis algorithm generates a random walk through configuration space that spends more time in low-energy states — exactly proportional to the Boltzmann weight. You don’t need to enumerate all configurations; you just sample the important ones. This is Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC).

import numpy as np

def ising_energy(spins, J=1):
    """Energy of a 1D Ising configuration."""
    return -J * np.sum(spins[:-1] * spins[1:])

# Examples
N = 10
all_up = np.ones(N, dtype=int)
alternating = np.array([(-1)**i for i in range(N)])
random_config = np.random.choice([-1, 1], N)

print(f'All up:       {all_up}  →  E = {ising_energy(all_up)}J')
print(f'Alternating:  {alternating}  →  E = {ising_energy(alternating)}J')
print(f'Random:       {random_config}  →  E = {ising_energy(random_config)}J')
print(f'\nGround state energy: E_min = {-(N-1)}J')
print(f'Max energy:          E_max = {+(N-1)}J')
print(f'Total configurations: 2^{N} = {2**N}')

import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go

def exact_ising_1d(N, T, J=1):
    """Exact enumeration of 1D Ising model."""
    energies, weights = [], []
    for config_int in range(2**N):
        # Convert integer to spin config
        spins = np.array([(config_int >> i) & 1 for i in range(N)])
        spins = 2 * spins - 1  # {0,1} → {-1,+1}
        E = -J * np.sum(spins[:-1] * spins[1:])
        w = np.exp(-E / T) if T > 0 else (1 if E == -(N-1)*J else 0)
        energies.append(E)
        weights.append(w)

    Z = sum(weights)
    probs = [w/Z for w in weights]
    avg_E = sum(E*p for E, p in zip(energies, probs))
    avg_M = sum(abs(sum([(config_int >> i) & 1 for i in range(N)])*2 - N) *
                probs[config_int] for config_int in range(2**N))
    return avg_E, avg_M, energies, probs

# Compute ⟨E⟩ vs T
N = 8
Ts = np.linspace(0.2, 5, 50)
avg_Es = [exact_ising_1d(N, T)[0] for T in Ts]

fig = go.Figure()
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=Ts, y=avg_Es, mode='lines',
    line=dict(color='#00f3ff', width=2.5), name='⟨E⟩'))
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=[0, 5], y=[-(N-1), -(N-1)],
    mode='lines', line=dict(color='#808080', dash='dot'),
    name='Ground state'))

fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark',
    xaxis=dict(title='T/J', zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    yaxis=dict(title='⟨E⟩/J', zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    height=400, width=550)
fig.show()
