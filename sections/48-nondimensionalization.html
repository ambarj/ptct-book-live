Non-dimensionalization

In Section 4.14, we derived the escape ODE: \( \ddot{x} = -gR^2/x^2 \). This equation has two parameters (\( g \) and \( R \)) that depend on which planet you're on. Can we make them disappear? The trick is to measure everything in natural units for the problem.

Learning Objectives
  • Choose natural scales (length \( R \), time \( \sqrt{R/g} \)) to non-dimensionalize the escape ODE
  • See that the dimensionless equation \( \ddot{X} = -1/X^2 \) has no free parameters
  • Discover that \( V_0 = \sqrt{2} \) is the universal escape threshold for any planet
  • Translate between dimensionless results and physical units for any celestial body

The scaling substitution:

$$ x = R\,X, \qquad t = \sqrt{\frac{R}{g}}\,T $$

where \( X \) is distance in units of the planet's radius, and \( T \) is time in units of the natural time scale \( \tau = \sqrt{R/g} \).

Substituting into the ODE:

$$ \frac{R}{\tau^2}\,\ddot{X} = -\frac{gR^2}{R^2 X^2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{R}{R/g}\,\ddot{X} = -\frac{g}{X^2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\ddot{X} = -\frac{1}{X^2}} $$
The Key Insight

The dimensionless equation \( \ddot{X} = -1/X^2 \) contains no parameters at all. The solution depends only on the initial conditions: \( X(0) = 1 \) (start at the surface) and \( \dot{X}(0) = V_0 \) (dimensionless launch speed). Every planet in the universe obeys the same equation — the physics is universal.

The dimensionless launch speed is:

$$ V_0 = \frac{v_0}{\sqrt{gR}} $$

The energy integral becomes \( \tfrac{1}{2}V^2 - 1/X = \tfrac{1}{2}V_0^2 - 1 = E \). Escape requires \( E \geq 0 \), giving the universal threshold \( V_0 \geq \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414 \).

The Universal Phase Portrait

The dimensionless energy integral gives us velocity as a function of position:

$$ V^2 = V_0^2 - 2\left(1 - \frac{1}{X}\right) = V_0^2 - 2 + \frac{2}{X} $$

The total energy (per unit mass, dimensionless) is \( E = \tfrac{1}{2}V_0^2 - 1 \).

Three regimes emerge:

  • \( V_0 < \sqrt{2} \) → \( E < 0 \) → Bound: the projectile turns around at \( X_{\max} = 1/(1 - V_0^2/2) \)
  • \( V_0 = \sqrt{2} \) → \( E = 0 \) → Critical: barely escapes, \( V \to 0 \) as \( X \to \infty \)
  • \( V_0 > \sqrt{2} \) → \( E > 0 \) → Unbound: escapes with speed \( V_\infty = \sqrt{V_0^2 - 2} \)

Translating Back to Physical Units

The power of non-dimensionalization is that you solve the problem once in dimensionless units, then translate the answer to any planet by plugging in that planet's scales:

The translation dictionary:

$$ v_0 = V_0 \sqrt{gR}, \qquad t_{\text{phys}} = T\sqrt{\frac{R}{g}}, \qquad x_{\text{phys}} = X \cdot R $$

For example, \( V_0 = \sqrt{2} \) (escape) translates to:

  • Earth: \( v_0 = \sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{9.8 \times 6.37 \times 10^6} \approx 11.2 \) km/s
  • Moon: \( v_0 = \sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{1.62 \times 1.74 \times 10^6} \approx 2.4 \) km/s
  • Jupiter: \( v_0 = \sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{24.79 \times 7.15 \times 10^7} \approx 59.5 \) km/s

  • Initial conditions still exist (\( X(0) = 1 \), \( \dot{X}(0) = V_0 \)). Non-dimensionalization reduces parameters, not boundary conditions.

Python Code

Python 

import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go
from plotly.subplots import make_subplots

# Planetary data: (g, R)
planets = {
    'Earth':   (9.80, 6.37e6),
    'Moon':    (1.62, 1.74e6),
    'Mars':    (3.72, 3.39e6),
}

ratio = 0.8  # v0/v_esc
fig = make_subplots(rows=1, cols=2,
    subplot_titles=['Dimensional v²(x)', 'Dimensionless V²(X)'])

colors = ['#00f3ff', '#808080', '#ff6b6b']

for i, (name, (g, R)) in enumerate(planets.items()):
    v_esc = np.sqrt(2 * g * R)
    v0 = ratio * v_esc

    # Dimensional
    x = np.linspace(R, 15 * R, 500)
    v2 = v0**2 - 2 * g * R * (1 - R / x)
    fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=x/1e6, y=v2/1e6,
        name=name, line=dict(color=colors[i], width=2)), row=1, col=1)

    # Dimensionless (all collapse!)
    X = np.linspace(1, 15, 500)
    V0 = v0 / np.sqrt(g * R)  # = ratio * sqrt(2)
    V2 = V0**2 - 2 * (1 - 1/X)
    fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=X, y=V2,
        name=name, line=dict(color=colors[i], width=2),
        showlegend=False), row=1, col=2)

fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark')
fig.update_xaxes(title='x (Mm)', linecolor='#808080', row=1, col=1)
fig.update_xaxes(title='X (planet radii)', linecolor='#808080', row=1, col=2)
fig.update_yaxes(title='v² (km/s)²', linecolor='#808080', row=1, col=1)
fig.update_yaxes(title='V² (dimensionless)', linecolor='#808080', row=1, col=2)
fig.show()

print(f"Dimensionless V0 = {ratio * np.sqrt(2):.3f}")
print(f"Escape threshold: V0 = sqrt(2) = {np.sqrt(2):.3f}")

import numpy as np

# Dimensionless result: escape requires V0 >= sqrt(2)
V0_escape = np.sqrt(2)

planets = {
    'Earth':   (9.80,  6.37e6),
    'Moon':    (1.62,  1.74e6),
    'Mars':    (3.72,  3.39e6),
    'Jupiter': (24.79, 7.15e7),
}

print("Scale Translation: V0 = sqrt(2) -> physical escape velocity")
print(f"{'Planet':10s} {'g (m/s²)':>10s} {'R (km)':>10s} {'tau (s)':>10s} "
      f"{'v_esc (km/s)':>12s}")
print("-" * 58)

for name, (g, R) in planets.items():
    tau = np.sqrt(R / g)                    # time scale
    v_phys = V0_escape * np.sqrt(g * R)     # physical velocity
    print(f"{name:10s} {g:10.2f} {R/1e3:10.0f} {tau:10.1f} {v_phys/1e3:12.1f}")

# The beauty: ONE dimensionless number (sqrt(2)) gives
# the escape velocity for EVERY planet!

What's Next?

Now we have a clean, parameter-free ODE: \( \ddot{X} = -1/X^2 \). Energy conservation tells us whether escape happens (\( V_0 \geq \sqrt{2} \)), but not how the trajectory unfolds in time — questions like "how long to reach the Moon?" need \( X(T) \). In Section 4.16, we'll unleash RK4 on this dimensionless system to compute full trajectories and compare with the analytical solution at the critical speed.
