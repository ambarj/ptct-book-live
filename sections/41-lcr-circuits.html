LCR Circuits: Electrical Oscillations

A charged capacitor in an LCR circuit is a damped oscillator: the charge sloshes back and forth between capacitor and inductor, while the resistor bleeds energy as heat. The governing equation is identical to the mechanical oscillator you already know.

Learning Objectives
  • Solve the LCR circuit equation numerically using vector Euler's method
  • Discover that a single non-dimensional parameter \( w = L/(R^2 C) \) controls the entire behavior
  • Track energy flow between capacitor, inductor, and resistor dissipation

LCR Circuit Equation:

$$ L\frac{d^2Q}{dt^2} + R\frac{dQ}{dt} + \frac{Q}{C} = 0 $$

Compare with the mechanical oscillator: \( m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + kx = 0 \). The mapping is \( L \leftrightarrow m \), \( R \leftrightarrow b \), \( 1/C \leftrightarrow k \).

After non-dimensionalizing (scaling time by \( RC \) and charge by \( Q_0 \)), the equation becomes \( \ddot{Q} + \dot{Q} + wQ = 0 \) where the single parameter \( w = L/(R^2 C) \) controls everything. The coupled first-order system is:

$$ \frac{dQ}{dt} = I, \qquad \frac{dI}{dt} = -wQ - I $$

Slowly decrease \( w \) from 10 toward 0. At approximately what value of \( w \) do oscillations disappear?
Explanation: The transition occurs at \( w = 1/4 \). The oscillation frequency \( \beta = \sqrt{w - 1/4} \) becomes zero at this point. For \( w < 1/4 \), the system is overdamped and decays without oscillation. This is exactly the same critical damping condition \( R^2 = 4L/C \) from circuit theory.
The Power of Non-Dimensionalization

An LCR circuit has three physical parameters (\( L, R, C \)), but after non-dimensionalization only one number matters: \( w = L/(R^2C) \). This is the ratio of the inductive time scale \( L/R \) to the capacitive time scale \( RC \). When \( w > 1/4 \): underdamped. When \( w = 1/4 \): critical. When \( w < 1/4 \): overdamped. One parameter, three regimes, complete classification.

At \( w = 0.25 \) (critical damping), which statement best describes the behavior?
Explanation: Critical damping is the boundary between oscillation and pure exponential decay. The solution \( Q(t) = (1 + t/2)e^{-t/2} \) reaches zero faster than any overdamped solution, but without overshooting (oscillating). This is the "Goldilocks" regime used in engineering for fast settling.
The critical damping condition in terms of circuit components is \( R^2 = 4L/C \). In the non-dimensional parameter, the critical value is \( w = \) .
Explanation: From \( R^2 = 4L/C \), divide both sides by \( R^2C \): \( 1 = 4L/(R^2C) = 4w \), so \( w = 1/4 = 0.25 \). The discriminant of the characteristic equation \( \lambda^2 + \lambda + w = 0 \) vanishes when \( 1 - 4w = 0 \).
Compare the settling time at \( w = 0.10 \) (overdamped) and \( w = 5 \) (underdamped). Which statements are correct? (Select all that apply)
Explanation:
  • Overdamped slower: True — overdamped solutions have a slow exponential tail that takes longer to decay than critical damping.
  • Higher w = faster: False — highly underdamped systems ring for many cycles before settling. The fastest settling is at \( w = 0.25 \).
  • Never reaches zero: False — underdamped solutions oscillate with decaying amplitude and approach zero asymptotically.
  • Critical = shortest: True — this is the defining property of critical damping — it's the fastest approach to zero without overshoot.

Set \( w = 5 \). At \( t = 0 \), all the energy is stored in the capacitor. Where does all the energy eventually end up?
Explanation: The resistor is the only energy sink. As \( t \to \infty \), both \( Q \to 0 \) and \( I \to 0 \), so capacitor and inductor energies vanish. All the initial energy \( E_0 = \frac{1}{2}wQ_0^2 \) is eventually converted to heat via Joule dissipation \( \int_0^\infty I^2\, dt = E_0 \).
With \( w = 5 \), the initial capacitor energy is \( E_C(0) = \frac{1}{2}w \cdot 1^2 \). What is \( E_C(0) \)? .
Explanation: \( E_C(0) = \frac{1}{2}wQ_0^2 = \frac{1}{2}(5)(1)^2 = 2.5 \). This is the total energy available for oscillation and eventual dissipation. The initial energy shown in the stats bar should match this value.
Which statements about energy in the LCR circuit are correct? (Select all that apply)
Explanation:
  • Exchange: True — charge flows from capacitor to inductor and back, converting between electric and magnetic field energy.
  • EC + EL conserved: False — this sum decreases due to resistive dissipation \( dE/dt = -I^2 \).
  • Including dissipated: True — total energy (stored + dissipated) equals the initial energy. This is energy conservation.
  • EL always greater: False — they oscillate, and at \( t = 0 \) the inductor energy is zero while capacitor energy is maximum.

Python Implementation

Python 
import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go
from plotly.subplots import make_subplots

def euler_system(f, y0, t_max, h):
    """Vector Euler solver for coupled ODEs."""
    t = [0.0]
    Y = [np.array(y0, dtype=float)]
    y = np.array(y0, dtype=float)
    for _ in range(int(t_max / h)):
        y = y + h * np.array(f(y, t[-1]))
        t.append(t[-1] + h)
        Y.append(y.copy())
    return np.array(t), np.array(Y)

# Non-dimensional LCR: dQ/dt = I, dI/dt = -wQ - I
w = 10.0  # L/(R^2 C)

def lcr(y, t):
    Q, I = y
    return [I, -w * Q - I]

t, Y = euler_system(lcr, [1.0, 0.0], 15, 0.01)
Q, I = Y[:, 0], Y[:, 1]

# Exact solution (underdamped: w > 0.25)
beta = np.sqrt(w - 0.25)
Q_exact = np.exp(-t/2) * (np.cos(beta*t) + np.sin(beta*t)/(2*beta))

fig = make_subplots(rows=1, cols=2,
    subplot_titles=['Charge Q(t)', 'Phase Portrait'])
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=t, y=Q, name='Euler Q',
    line=dict(color='#00f3ff', width=2)), row=1, col=1)
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=t, y=Q_exact, name='Exact',
    line=dict(color='#808080', width=2, dash='dash')), row=1, col=1)
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=Q, y=I, name='(Q, I)',
    line=dict(color='#00ff9f', width=2)), row=1, col=2)

fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark')
fig.show()
Python 
import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go

def euler_system(f, y0, t_max, h):
    t = [0.0]
    Y = [np.array(y0, dtype=float)]
    y = np.array(y0, dtype=float)
    for _ in range(int(t_max / h)):
        y = y + h * np.array(f(y, t[-1]))
        t.append(t[-1] + h)
        Y.append(y.copy())
    return np.array(t), np.array(Y)

w = 5.0
def lcr(y, t):
    return [y[1], -w * y[0] - y[1]]

t, Y = euler_system(lcr, [1.0, 0.0], 15, 0.01)
Q, I = Y[:, 0], Y[:, 1]

# Energy components
E_cap = 0.5 * w * Q**2          # Capacitor energy
E_ind = 0.5 * I**2              # Inductor energy
E_total = E_cap + E_ind         # Total stored

# Cumulative dissipated energy
h_val = t[1] - t[0]
E_diss = np.cumsum(I**2 * h_val)  # integral of I^2 dt

fig = go.Figure()
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=t, y=E_cap, name='Capacitor',
    fill='tozeroy', line=dict(color='#00f3ff')))
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=t, y=E_ind, name='Inductor',
    fill='tozeroy', line=dict(color='#ff00ff')))
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=t, y=E_total, name='Total Stored',
    line=dict(color='#ffff00', width=2)))
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=t, y=E_diss, name='Dissipated',
    line=dict(color='#ff6b6b', width=2, dash='dash')))

fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark',
    xaxis=dict(title='t', linecolor='#808080'),
    yaxis=dict(title='Energy', linecolor='#808080'))
fig.show()
