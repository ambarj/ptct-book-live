The Effective Potential

Section 8.3 ended with a striking observation: the radial equation \( \mu\ddot\rho = -dU/d\rho + \ell^2/(\mu\rho^3) \) looks like one-dimensional Newtonian motion in some new potential. We can make this exact. Define

$$ \boxed{U_{\text{eff}}(\rho) = U(\rho) + \frac{\ell^2}{2\mu\rho^2}} $$

and check that

$$ -\frac{dU_{\text{eff}}}{d\rho} = -\frac{dU}{d\rho} + \frac{\ell^2}{\mu\rho^3} $$

— exactly the right-hand side of the radial equation. So

$$ \mu\ddot\rho = -\frac{dU_{\text{eff}}}{d\rho}, \qquad E = \tfrac{1}{2}\mu\dot\rho^2 + U_{\text{eff}}(\rho) $$

is a 1-D problem: a particle of mass μ moving along the ρ-axis in the potential \( U_{\text{eff}} \), with total energy E.

Once you have a 1-D problem with constant total energy, you have a powerful pedagogical tool: the energy diagram. Sketch \( U_{\text{eff}}(\rho) \), draw a horizontal line at E, and read off everything by inspection — turning points, allowed regions, circular-orbit radii, bound vs unbound. No ODE, no trajectory integral. Just a picture.

💡
Learning Objectives:
  • Define the effective potential \( U_{\text{eff}}(\rho) = U(\rho) + \ell^2/(2\mu\rho^2) \) and recognize it as the 1-D effective dynamics of the radial equation
  • Use the energy-diagram method: turning points where \( U_{\text{eff}}(\rho) = E \), allowed region where \( U_{\text{eff}} \le E \)
  • Locate the circular-orbit radius \( \rho_{\text{circ}} \) at the minimum of \( U_{\text{eff}} \), and compute \( E_{\text{min}} = U_{\text{eff}}(\rho_{\text{circ}}) \)
  • Classify orbits by E and ℓ: forbidden, circular, bound, parabolic, unbound (hyperbolic) for Kepler; always bound for SHO
  • Apply the same pictorial method to any central potential — the recipe is independent of the specific U(ρ)

The Energy-Diagram Recipe

Here is the seven-step recipe for extracting orbital dynamics from a single plot of \( U_{\text{eff}} \):

  1. Sketch \( U_{\text{eff}}(\rho) \) for the chosen \( U(\rho) \) and value of \( \ell \).
  2. Draw a horizontal line at total energy \( E \).
  3. Turning points: values of \( \rho \) where \( U_{\text{eff}}(\rho) = E \). At a turning point, \( \dot\rho = 0 \) and the radial motion reverses.
  4. Allowed region: the interval(s) of \( \rho \) where \( U_{\text{eff}}(\rho) \le E \). Outside this region the radial kinetic energy would be negative — physically forbidden.
  5. Circular orbit: when \( E \) equals the minimum of \( U_{\text{eff}} \), the only "allowed" \( \rho \) is the minimum itself. The particle stays at fixed radius — a circular orbit. The condition \( dU_{\text{eff}}/d\rho = 0 \) gives \( \rho_{\text{circ}} \).
  6. Bound orbit: if \( E \) lies between \( E_{\text{min}} \) and the asymptotic value \( U_{\text{eff}}(\rho \to \infty) \), the allowed region is a finite interval \( [\rho_{\text{min}}, \rho_{\text{max}}] \) and the orbit oscillates radially while sweeping in \( \phi \). For Kepler this gives ellipses.
  7. Unbound orbit: if \( E \) exceeds the asymptotic value, one of the turning points is at infinity and the particle escapes. For Kepler at \( E = 0 \) the orbit is a parabola; at \( E > 0 \), a hyperbola.
💡
Pedagogical payoff: total energy = radial KE + U_eff.

The angular kinetic energy \( \tfrac{1}{2}\mu\rho^2\dot\phi^2 \) has been absorbed into the centrifugal term \( \ell^2/(2\mu\rho^2) \) inside \( U_{\text{eff}} \). What's left is just radial motion. Reading an orbit from a U_eff plot is identical to reading a 1-D ball-rolling-in-a-bowl problem from elementary mechanics.

💡
Computational Thinking: A Picture Solves the Problem.

We just classified every possible orbit in central-force motion using nothing more than a 1-D plot. No analytical integration. No numerical integration. The pictorial method scales far beyond Kepler and SHO: it works for the Yukawa potential (screened nuclear forces), the Lennard-Jones potential (intermolecular interactions), empirically fit potentials, even potentials that have no closed-form solution. Section 8.5 will solve the trajectory analytically for the special case \( U = -k/\rho \) (Kepler ellipses); Section 8.6 will integrate arbitrary central-force orbits numerically. But the qualitative picture from \( U_{\text{eff}} \) is independent of either approach — and often it's all you need.

import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go
from scipy.optimize import minimize_scalar

mu, k, ell = 1.0, 1.0, 1.0

def U(rho):       return -k / rho                        # bare Kepler
def Ucent(rho):   return ell**2 / (2 * mu * rho**2)      # centrifugal
def Ueff(rho):    return U(rho) + Ucent(rho)

# Find the minimum numerically (also has a closed form for Kepler)
res = minimize_scalar(Ueff, bounds=(0.05, 10), method='bounded')
rho_circ, Emin = res.x, res.fun
print(f"ρ_circ = {rho_circ:.4f}  (theory ℓ²/(μk) = {ell**2/(mu*k):.4f})")
print(f"E_min  = {Emin:.4f}  (theory -μk²/(2ℓ²) = {-mu*k**2/(2*ell**2):.4f})")

rho = np.linspace(0.2, 8, 500)
fig = go.Figure()
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=rho, y=U(rho),     mode='lines',
    line=dict(color='#00f3ff', width=1.5, dash='dot'), name='U(ρ) = -k/ρ'))
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=rho, y=Ucent(rho), mode='lines',
    line=dict(color='#ffbe0b', width=1.5, dash='dot'), name='ℓ²/(2μρ²)'))
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=rho, y=Ueff(rho),  mode='lines',
    line=dict(color='#ff006e', width=2.5),             name='U_eff'))
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=[rho_circ], y=[Emin], mode='markers',
    marker=dict(color='#00ff88', size=14, symbol='diamond'),
    name=f'min at ρ = {rho_circ:.2f}'))
fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark',
    xaxis=dict(title='ρ', range=[0, 8], zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    yaxis=dict(title='energy', range=[-1.5, 1.5], zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    height=420)
fig.show()

import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import brentq

def classify_orbit(E, ell, mu, k, potential='kepler'):
    """Return classification + turning points by inspecting U_eff."""
    if potential == 'kepler':
        U     = lambda r: -k / r
        Uinf  = 0
    else:                          # 'sho'
        U     = lambda r: 0.5 * k * r * r
        Uinf  = np.inf
    Ueff   = lambda r: U(r) + ell**2 / (2 * mu * r * r)
    g      = lambda r: Ueff(r) - E   # roots = turning points

    # Locate U_eff minimum via dU_eff/dρ = 0
    if potential == 'kepler':
        rho_circ = ell**2 / (mu * k)
        Emin     = -mu * k**2 / (2 * ell**2)
    else:
        rho_circ = (ell**2 / (mu * k)) ** 0.25
        Emin     = ell * np.sqrt(k / mu)

    if E < Emin - 1e-9:                        return 'forbidden', []
    if abs(E - Emin) < 1e-6:                   return 'circular',  [rho_circ]

    # Find turning points by bracketing roots of g(r) = 0
    rmin = brentq(g, 0.01,        rho_circ)
    if E >= Uinf - 1e-9:
        return 'unbound', [rmin]
    rmax = brentq(g, rho_circ, 100)
    return 'bound', [rmin, rmax]

# Demo
for E in [-0.6, -0.5, -0.25, 0.0, 0.1]:
    cls, tps = classify_orbit(E, ell=1, mu=1, k=1, potential='kepler')
    print(f"E = {E:+.2f}: {cls:10s}  turning points = {[round(t, 3) for t in tps]}")

import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go

# Plot four characteristic Kepler orbits using closed-form conic-section
# r(φ) = p / (1 + e cos φ), where
#   p = ℓ²/(μk),  e = sqrt(1 + 2 E ℓ²/(μ k²))
mu, k, ell = 1.0, 1.0, 1.0
p = ell**2 / (mu * k)

cases = [
    ('E = E_min (circle)',   -0.5, '#00f3ff'),
    ('E = E_min/2 (ellipse)', -0.25, '#00ff88'),
    ('E = 0 (parabola)',       0.0, '#ffbe0b'),
    ('E = +0.1 (hyperbola)',   0.1, '#ff006e'),
]
fig = go.Figure()
for label, E, color in cases:
    e2 = 1 + 2 * E * ell**2 / (mu * k**2)
    e  = np.sqrt(max(e2, 0))
    # Limit φ to keep ρ positive for hyperbola
    if e < 1:    phi = np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, 400)
    elif e == 1: phi = np.linspace(-np.pi*0.99, np.pi*0.99, 400)
    else:
        phi_max = np.arccos(-1/e) * 0.99
        phi     = np.linspace(-phi_max, phi_max, 400)
    rho = p / (1 + e * np.cos(phi))
    x, y = rho * np.cos(phi), rho * np.sin(phi)
    fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=x, y=y, mode='lines',
        line=dict(color=color, width=2), name=label))

fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark',
    xaxis=dict(range=[-5, 5], zerolinecolor='#808080', scaleanchor='y'),
    yaxis=dict(range=[-5, 5], zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    title='Kepler orbits across all four energy regimes',
    height=500)
fig.show()
