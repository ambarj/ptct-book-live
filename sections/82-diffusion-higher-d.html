Higher Dimensions: 2D & 3D Diffusion

Real diffusion happens in 2D and 3D: ink spreading in water, heat flowing through a metal plate, molecules diffusing through a cell. The diffusion equation generalizes naturally: replace \( \partial^2/\partial x^2 \) with the Laplacian \( \nabla^2 \). The Gaussian solution becomes a circular (2D) or spherical (3D) cloud, and the RMS displacement picks up a factor of \( \sqrt{d} \) for dimension \( d \).

Learning Objectives
  • Write the diffusion equation in \( d \) dimensions: \( \partial\rho/\partial t = D\nabla^2\rho \)
  • Know the \( d \)-dimensional Gaussian: \( \rho = (4\pi Dt)^{-d/2}\exp(-r^2/(4Dt)) \)
  • Compute the RMS displacement: \( \sqrt{\langle r^2 \rangle} = \sqrt{2dDt} \) (factor of \( \sqrt{d} \) from 1D)
  • Visualize 2D diffusion as a spreading circular cloud

Diffusion in \( d \) Dimensions:

$$ \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} = D\nabla^2\rho, \qquad \rho(\mathbf{r}, t) = \frac{1}{(4\pi Dt)^{d/2}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{r^2}{4Dt}\right) $$

RMS displacement: \( \sqrt{\langle r^2 \rangle} = \sqrt{2dDt} \). In 1D: \( \sqrt{2Dt} \). In 2D: \( \sqrt{4Dt} \). In 3D: \( \sqrt{6Dt} \).

The 2D diffusion cloud is shaped like…
Explanation: The 2D Gaussian \( \rho \propto e^{-(x^2+y^2)/(4Dt)} \) has circular symmetry. The contours are circles centered at the origin, with radius growing as \( \sqrt{t} \).
In 2D with D = 1, t = 1: the RMS distance is \( \sqrt{2 \times 2 \times 1 \times 1} = \sqrt{4} = \) .
Explanation: \( \sqrt{\langle r^2 \rangle} = \sqrt{2dDt} = \sqrt{2 \times 2 \times 1 \times 1} = \sqrt{4} = 2 \). Compare with 1D: \( \sqrt{2 \times 1} \approx 1.41 \). Higher dimensions spread farther because there are more directions to explore.
The peak of the 2D Gaussian decreases as \( 1/t \) (not \( 1/\sqrt{t} \) like 1D). Why?
Explanation: Peak \( = (4\pi Dt)^{-d/2} \). In 1D: \( \propto t^{-1/2} \). In 2D: \( \propto t^{-1} \). In 3D: \( \propto t^{-3/2} \). Higher dimensions dilute the peak faster because probability spreads into more directions (area grows as \( t \), volume as \( t^{3/2} \)).

Explanation: 2D diffusion describes spreading in a plane: ink on a surface, heat in a thin plate, molecules diffusing on a cell membrane. The circular Gaussian cloud is the universal shape of 2D diffusion from a point source.

Python Code

Python

import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go

D, t = 1.0, 1.0
n = 100
x = np.linspace(-5, 5, n)
y = np.linspace(-5, 5, n)
X, Y = np.meshgrid(x, y)
R2 = X**2 + Y**2
rho = np.exp(-R2 / (4*D*t)) / (4*np.pi*D*t)

fig = go.Figure(go.Heatmap(
    z=rho, x=x, y=y,
    colorscale='Viridis', showscale=True))
fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark',
    xaxis=dict(title='x', zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    yaxis=dict(title='y', zerolinecolor='#808080',
        scaleanchor='x'),
    height=500, width=550)
fig.show()
Python

import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go

D, t = 1.0, 1.0
r = np.linspace(0, 6, 200)

fig = go.Figure()
for d, color, label in [(1,'#00f3ff','1D'), (2,'#ffbe0b','2D'), (3,'#ff006e','3D')]:
    rho = np.exp(-r**2 / (4*D*t)) / (4*np.pi*D*t)**(d/2)
    fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=r, y=rho, mode='lines',
        line=dict(color=color, width=2.5), name=f'{label}: peak={(4*np.pi*D*t)**(-d/2):.3f}'))

fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark',
    xaxis=dict(title='r', zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    yaxis=dict(title='ρ(r,t)', zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    height=400, width=550)
fig.show()
