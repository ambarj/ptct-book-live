Resonance Disasters and Designs

Resonance is not just a textbook curiosity — it can bring down bridges or let you tune in to your favourite radio station. The same physics that makes a wine glass shatter also lets an MRI machine image your brain. In this section we explore both sides of resonance: catastrophic failures when it strikes where you don’t want it, and elegant designs that harness it on purpose.

The key insight is always the same: a small periodic force, applied at the right frequency, can produce an enormous response. Whether that’s a disaster or a triumph depends entirely on whether you planned for it.

Learning Objectives
  • Explain why resonance can be destructive (bridges, buildings) or useful (radio, MRI)
  • Design a tuned mass damper (TMD) to suppress unwanted resonance
  • Use the Q factor and bandwidth to select a specific frequency from a mixture
  • Apply the “avoid or exploit?” decision framework to new engineering problems

The resonance amplification factor:

$$ A(\omega_d) = \frac{F_0/m}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \omega_d^2)^2 + (2\gamma\omega_d)^2}} $$

At resonance (\( \omega_d \approx \omega_0 \)), the denominator simplifies and the peak amplitude becomes \( A_{\text{peak}} \approx F_0 / (2m\gamma\omega_0) \). Since \( Q = \omega_0/(2\gamma) \), this is the same as \( Q \cdot F_0/(m\omega_0^2) \) — the response is amplified by a factor of Q. High Q means huge amplification: great for sensors, terrible for structures.

The Engineering Lesson

Every structure has a natural frequency. If an external periodic force happens to match that frequency, the response grows dangerously. Strategy 1: Add damping (lower Q, reduce the peak). Strategy 2: Shift the frequency (change mass or stiffness so \( \omega_0 \) moves away from the driving frequency). Strategy 3: Attach a tuned mass damper (see Explorer 2).

Rescue the Bridge: Tuned Mass Dampers

A tuned mass damper (TMD) is a secondary oscillator bolted to the primary structure, tuned to the same natural frequency. When the structure starts vibrating, the TMD absorbs energy and converts the single resonance peak into two smaller peaks — like splitting a dangerous mountain road into two gentle hills. The Taipei 101 skyscraper uses a 730-tonne TMD to survive typhoons and earthquakes.

Adjust the mass ratio \( \mu = m_{\text{TMD}} / m_{\text{structure}} \) and the TMD damping \( \gamma_{\text{TMD}} \). Watch how the single resonance peak splits and shrinks. The bar chart shows the reduction factor.

When Resonance is Your Friend: The Radio Tuner

Not all resonance is bad. A radio receiver uses an LC circuit whose resonant frequency \( \omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC} \) is tunable. When you “turn the dial,” you’re adjusting \( \omega_0 \) to match the station’s broadcast frequency. The circuit’s sharp resonance peak amplifies the desired signal while rejecting nearby frequencies — the higher the Q, the better the selectivity.

Three stations broadcast at different frequencies. Adjust \( \omega_0 \) to “tune in” to each one. Increase Q to sharpen the response curve and reject neighboring stations more effectively.

Avoid or Exploit? The Decision Framework

When you encounter resonance in the wild, ask two questions:

  • Is resonance helpful? (Radio tuning, musical instruments, MRI, clocks.) → Maximize Q, tune \( \omega_0 \) precisely.
  • Is resonance harmful? (Bridges, buildings, aircraft wings, washing machines.) → Add damping, install TMDs, or redesign to shift \( \omega_0 \) away from expected driving frequencies.

import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go
from plotly.subplots import make_subplots

omega0, F0m = 3.0, 1.0
gammas = [0.05, 0.10, 0.30, 0.50]
omega_d = np.linspace(0.1, 6, 500)

fig = make_subplots(rows=1, cols=2,
    subplot_titles=['Amplitude Response', 'Peak vs Damping'])

colors = ['#ff6b6b', '#00f3ff', '#00ff9f', '#ffff00']
peaks = []

for gamma, color in zip(gammas, colors):
    denom = np.sqrt((omega0**2 - omega_d**2)**2 + (2*gamma*omega_d)**2)
    A = F0m / denom
    peak = F0m / (2*gamma*omega0)
    peaks.append(peak)
    fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=omega_d, y=A, mode='lines',
        line=dict(color=color, width=2),
        name=f'γ={gamma}'), row=1, col=1)

# Danger threshold
fig.add_hline(y=3.0, line_dash='dot', line_color='#ff4444',
    annotation_text='Danger', row=1, col=1)

# Peak amplitude vs damping
fig.add_trace(go.Bar(x=[f'γ={g}' for g in gammas], y=peaks,
    marker_color=colors), row=1, col=2)
fig.add_hline(y=3.0, line_dash='dot', line_color='#ff4444', row=1, col=2)

fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark', showlegend=True,
    legend=dict(x=0.02, y=0.98))
fig.update_xaxes(title_text='ω_d (rad/s)', row=1, col=1,
    gridcolor='#2a2f4a', zerolinecolor='#808080', linecolor='#808080')
fig.update_yaxes(title_text='Amplitude', range=[0, 15], row=1, col=1,
    gridcolor='#2a2f4a', zerolinecolor='#808080', linecolor='#808080')
fig.update_yaxes(title_text='Peak amplitude', row=1, col=2,
    gridcolor='#2a2f4a', zerolinecolor='#808080', linecolor='#808080')
fig.show()
Python 
import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go

omega0 = 3.0
F0m = 1.0
g1 = 0.05  # primary damping
omega_d = np.linspace(0.1, 6, 500)

def tmd_response(omega_d, mu, g2):
    """Two-DOF amplitude with tuned mass damper."""
    w2 = omega_d**2
    w02 = omega0**2
    wt2 = omega0**2  # TMD tuned to primary
    numR = wt2 - w2
    numI = 2 * g2 * omega_d
    num2 = numR**2 + numI**2
    DR = (w02 - w2)*(wt2 - w2) - mu*wt2*w2 - 4*g1*g2*w2
    DI = 2*omega_d*(g2*(w02 - w2) + g1*(wt2 - w2) + mu*g2*w2)
    D2 = DR**2 + DI**2
    return F0m * np.sqrt(num2 / np.maximum(D2, 1e-20))

# No TMD
A_no_tmd = F0m / np.sqrt((omega0**2 - omega_d**2)**2 + (2*g1*omega_d)**2)

fig = go.Figure()
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=omega_d, y=A_no_tmd, mode='lines',
    line=dict(color='#ff6b6b', width=2, dash='dash'), name='No TMD'))

for mu, color in [(0.02, '#ffff00'), (0.05, '#00f3ff'), (0.10, '#00ff9f')]:
    A_tmd = tmd_response(omega_d, mu, g2=0.2)
    fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=omega_d, y=A_tmd, mode='lines',
        line=dict(color=color, width=2), name=f'TMD μ={mu}'))

fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark',
    title='TMD Effect on Resonance Peak',
    xaxis=dict(title='ω_d (rad/s)', range=[0, 6],
        gridcolor='#2a2f4a', zerolinecolor='#808080', linecolor='#808080'),
    yaxis=dict(title='Amplitude', range=[0, 15],
        gridcolor='#2a2f4a', zerolinecolor='#808080', linecolor='#808080'),
    legend=dict(x=0.6, y=0.98, bgcolor='rgba(0,0,0,0.3)'))
fig.show()
