Introducing Damping: Two Time Scales

Every real oscillation eventually stops. A guitar string falls silent, a pendulum comes to rest, an LC circuit's current dies away. Where does the energy go? It's lost to friction, air resistance, or electrical resistance — all forms of damping that convert orderly oscillation into heat.

The key insight is that damping introduces a second time scale. Instead of one number (\( \omega_0 \)) describing the system, you now need two: how fast it oscillates, and how fast it fades. The competition between these two time scales determines everything.

Learning Objectives
  • Write the damped oscillator equation \( \ddot{x} + 2\gamma\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = 0 \) and identify each term
  • Understand \( \gamma \) as a damping rate: amplitude decays as \( e^{-\gamma t} \)
  • Distinguish the oscillation time scale \( T_\text{osc} \) from the damping time scale \( \tau_\text{damp} = 1/\gamma \)
  • Estimate damping from observed motion: count oscillations before fading

The damped harmonic oscillator:

$$ \ddot{x} + 2\gamma\dot{x} + \omega_0^2 x = 0 $$

Three terms, each with a physical role: \( \omega_0^2 x \) is the restoring force (pulls back), \( 2\gamma\dot{x} \) is the damping force (opposes motion), and \( \ddot{x} \) is the inertia. The solution (for light damping, \( \gamma < \omega_0 \)):

$$ x(t) = A\,e^{-\gamma t}\cos(\omega_d t + \varphi), \qquad \omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2} $$

The Two Time Scales

The essential physics of damping is the competition between two time scales:

$$ T_\text{osc} = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_d} \quad \text{(how long one oscillation takes)} $$ $$ \tau_\text{damp} = \frac{1}{\gamma} \quad \text{(how long the envelope takes to decay)} $$

Their ratio \( \tau/T \) tells the whole story. If \( \tau \gg T \), the system completes many oscillations before the envelope noticeably shrinks — this is light damping. If \( \tau \approx T \), you barely see one oscillation before it's gone — heavy damping. The green and yellow lines on the plot mark these two time scales.

Explorer 2: Two Time Scales
Key Insight

The quality factor \( Q = \omega_0/(2\gamma) \) is a handy single number that tells you: how many oscillations does the system complete before the motion dies out? A tuning fork has Q ≈ 1000 (it rings for a long time). A car suspension has Q ≈ 5 (it’s designed to stop bouncing quickly). You can read Q directly from the explorer: count how many full oscillations are visible before the envelope drops below ~37%.

Explanation
\( Q = \omega_0/(2\gamma) \), so \( \gamma = \omega_0/(2Q) = 2\pi \times 32768 / (2 \times 10000) \approx 10.3 \) s\(^{-1}\). Explorer 3: Damping Detector
Key Insight

Estimating damping from observation is a real experimental skill. Here’s the trick: measure two consecutive peak amplitudes \( A_n \) and \( A_{n+1} \), then compute their ratio. Since the envelope decays exponentially, this ratio is the same for any two consecutive peaks: \( A_n/A_{n+1} = e^{\gamma T} \). Taking the natural log gives \( \ln(A_n/A_{n+1}) = \gamma T \), and since you can measure the period T, you get \( \gamma \) directly.

Code It Yourself

Python 
import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go

A, omega0, gamma = 2.0, 3.0, 0.2
omega_d = np.sqrt(omega0**2 - gamma**2)
t = np.linspace(0, 20, 800)

x = A * np.exp(-gamma * t) * np.cos(omega_d * t)
env = A * np.exp(-gamma * t)

fig = go.Figure()
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=t, y=x, name='Damped',
    line=dict(color='#00f3ff', width=2)))
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=t, y=env, name='Envelope',
    line=dict(color='#ff6b6b', width=1.5, dash='dash')))
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=t, y=-env, showlegend=False,
    line=dict(color='#ff6b6b', width=1.5, dash='dash')))

# Mark tau = 1/gamma
tau = 1/gamma
fig.add_vline(x=tau, line=dict(color='#ffff00', dash='dashdot'),
    annotation_text=f'τ = {tau:.1f} s')

fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark',
    title=f'Damped Oscillator: γ={gamma}, ω₀={omega0}',
    xaxis=dict(title='Time (s)', range=[0, 20],
        gridcolor='#2a2f4a', zerolinecolor='#808080', linecolor='#808080'),
    yaxis=dict(title='x(t)', range=[-2.5, 2.5],
        gridcolor='#2a2f4a', zerolinecolor='#808080', linecolor='#808080'))
fig.show()

import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go

omega0 = 3.0
gammas = np.linspace(0.01, 2.5, 200)

T_osc = 2 * np.pi / np.sqrt(np.maximum(omega0**2 - gammas**2, 0.01))
tau_damp = 1.0 / gammas
ratio = tau_damp / T_osc

fig = go.Figure()
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=gammas, y=T_osc, name='T_osc',
    line=dict(color='#00ff9f', width=2)))
fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=gammas, y=tau_damp, name='τ_damp',
    line=dict(color='#ffff00', width=2)))
fig.add_hline(y=1, line=dict(color='#808080', dash='dot'))

# Shade the crossover region
fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark',
    title='Time Scales vs Damping',
    xaxis=dict(title='γ (s⁻¹)', range=[0, 2.5],
        gridcolor='#2a2f4a', zerolinecolor='#808080', linecolor='#808080'),
    yaxis=dict(title='Time (s)', range=[0, 20], type='linear',
        gridcolor='#2a2f4a', zerolinecolor='#808080', linecolor='#808080'))
fig.show()
