The Diffusion Equation & Gaussian Solutions

The previous section derived the diffusion equation \( \partial\rho/\partial t = D\,\partial^2\rho/\partial x^2 \). Now we solve it. For a point source at the origin, the solution is a Gaussian that spreads over time: tall and narrow at early times, short and wide at late times. The width grows as \( \sqrt{t} \) — exactly the same \( \sqrt{N} \) scaling from random walks, now emerging from a PDE.

Learning Objectives
  • Know the fundamental solution: \( \rho(x,t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi Dt}}\,e^{-x^2/(4Dt)} \)
  • Verify that the width grows as \( \sigma(t) = \sqrt{2Dt} \) and the peak decreases as \( 1/\sqrt{t} \)
  • Understand conservation of probability: \( \int \rho\,dx = 1 \) for all time
  • Explore how different initial conditions (step, double peak) evolve under diffusion

The Fundamental Solution (Green’s Function):

$$ \rho(x, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi D t}}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{4Dt}\right) $$

This is the probability density of finding a particle at position \( x \) at time \( t \), given it started at the origin. Width: \( \sigma = \sqrt{2Dt} \). Peak: \( \rho(0,t) = 1/\sqrt{4\pi Dt} \propto 1/\sqrt{t} \).

Drag the time slider to watch the Gaussian spread. At \( t = 0 \) the distribution is a sharp peak (delta function). As time advances, it broadens and lowers — but the area under the curve stays exactly 1.

Set D = 1 and move time from 0.1 to 2.0. How does the peak height change?
Explanation: \( \rho(0,t) = 1/\sqrt{4\pi Dt} \propto 1/\sqrt{t} \). As the Gaussian widens, it must lower to keep the total area = 1. The probability spreads out but is never created or destroyed.
With D = 1, find the time at which the profile’s width reaches σ = 3; check the plot width. .
Explanation: From \( \sigma = \sqrt{2Dt} \), set \( 3 = \sqrt{2t} \), so \( 9 = 2t \) and \( t = 4.5 \). Slide the time to 4.5 and the Gaussian width readout hits 3. Reaching width 3 takes longer than reaching width 2 (t = 2) — the \( \sqrt{t} \) law means later gains come slower.
Which properties of the diffusion solution are true? (Select all)
Explanation:
  • Conservation: ✓ The diffusion equation conserves total probability (integral of ρ is constant).
  • Delta at large t: ✗ The opposite: the solution spreads out and flattens. At t → ∞, ρ → 0 everywhere (uniform spread).
  • Width ∝ √t: ✓ σ = √(2Dt). Doubling the width takes 4× the time.
  • Center moves: ✗ For unbiased diffusion, the center stays at x = 0. Drift would require an extra term in the PDE.

On the σ vs t plot, what shape is the curve?
Explanation: \( \sigma = \sqrt{2Dt} \) is a square root curve. Fast initial growth that slows down — the same √N from random walks, now from the PDE. Diffusion is fast at short times, slow at long times.
To triple the width, how many times longer must you wait? × as long.
Explanation: Since \( \sigma \propto \sqrt{t} \), tripling the width means \( 3\sigma \propto \sqrt{9t} \) — you must wait 9 times as long. The cost grows as the square of the width factor, which is why diffusion is quick at small scales but hopeless over large ones.
Increase D from 1 to 2. How does the σ vs t curve change?
Explanation: \( \sigma = \sqrt{2Dt} \). Larger D → larger σ at the same t. The curve is steeper: the distribution spreads faster. Physically, a larger diffusion constant means the microscopic steps are bigger or faster.
Explorer: Different Starting Distributions

Diffusion doesn’t just apply to point sources. Start with a step function, a double peak, or a square pulse — watch each smooth out over time. Diffusion erases structure: sharp features are destroyed first, broad features persist longer.

Select Step function and advance time. What happens to the sharp edge?
Explanation: Diffusion smooths sharp features. The step function evolves into an error function (complementary error function, erfc). The transition region widens as √t. This is exactly what happens when you open a door between a hot room and a cold one — the temperature gradient smooths out diffusively.
Select Double peak and advance time. What happens to the two peaks?
Explanation: Each peak spreads independently as a Gaussian. Once the widths exceed the separation, they overlap and merge into a single broad bump. Diffusion erases the memory of the initial two-peak structure. The time to merge depends on the initial separation and D.
Two profiles have equal area: one tall and narrow, one low and broad. Which loses its shape first?
Explanation: The diffusion rate \( \partial\rho/\partial t = D\,\partial^2\rho/\partial x^2 \) is proportional to curvature. A tall narrow spike has enormous curvature, so it decays first; a low broad bump is nearly flat and barely changes. Try the Square pulse initial condition at small t: its sharp edges round off almost immediately while the interior lags behind.

Python Code

Python 

import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go

D = 1.0
x = np.linspace(-8, 8, 500)

fig = go.Figure()
times = [0.1, 0.3, 1.0, 3.0]
colors = ['#ff006e', '#ffbe0b', '#00f3ff', '#00ff88']

for t, color in zip(times, colors):
    rho = np.exp(-x**2 / (4*D*t)) / np.sqrt(4*np.pi*D*t)
    sigma = np.sqrt(2*D*t)
    fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=x, y=rho, mode='lines',
        line=dict(color=color, width=2),
        name=f't={t} (σ={sigma:.2f})'))

fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark',
    xaxis=dict(title='x', range=[-8, 8], zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    yaxis=dict(title='ρ(x,t)', range=[0, 1.5], zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    height=450, width=600)
fig.show()
Python 

import numpy as np
import plotly.graph_objects as go
from scipy.special import erfc

D = 1.0
x = np.linspace(-8, 8, 500)

# Step function IC: rho(x,0) = 1 for x<0, 0 for x>0
# Solution: rho(x,t) = 0.5 * erfc(x / sqrt(4Dt))

fig = go.Figure()
times = [0.01, 0.1, 0.5, 2.0]
colors = ['#ff006e', '#ffbe0b', '#00f3ff', '#00ff88']

for t, color in zip(times, colors):
    rho = 0.5 * erfc(x / np.sqrt(4*D*t))
    fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(x=x, y=rho, mode='lines',
        line=dict(color=color, width=2), name=f't={t}'))

fig.update_layout(template='plotly_dark',
    xaxis=dict(title='x', range=[-8, 8], zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    yaxis=dict(title='ρ(x,t)', range=[-0.05, 1.1], zerolinecolor='#808080'),
    height=450, width=600)
fig.show()
